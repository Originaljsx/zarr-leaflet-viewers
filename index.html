Zarr Leaflet Viewers

Leaflet viewers rendering multiscale zarr v3 pyramids directly in the browser, streamed from CloudFront. No backend, no tiles server. The current viewers render through zarr-gl, a custom WebGL layer with one shared canvas and per-tile textures — no tile-edge seams and no antimeridian/drift issues. A couple of early pages still render through the older zarr-maps fork (L.GridLayer, one canvas per tile) and are kept only as before/after comparisons, linked at the bottom.

  • SWOT SSHA — Expert 2 km / Unsmoothed 250 m
    sea-surface height anomaly swaths, cycle 42; slice slider, click for value. Through zarr-gl: one shared canvas and GL context, per-tile textures, no tile seams
  • MUR SST 1 km — Nov–Dec 2025
    global sea-surface temperature + anomaly; day slider, click for value. Through zarr-gl: one canvas, per-tile textures, Mercator reprojection in the shader — no antimeridian gap, no north-south drift on zoom
  • SWOT SSHA — EPSG:3413 polar stereographic
    SWOT data reprojected to true polar stereographic (not fake-Mercator), also through zarr-gl, with pass/day/cycle mode switching and a bottom timeline control
  • Planet Tanager hyperspectral — 426 bands, EPSG:3413
    surface reflectance from an Icechunk store, reprojected to polar stereographic; band slider steps 376–2499 nm client-side, clicking the scene plots the pixel's full spectrum
  • Planet Tanager hyperspectral — Singrauli coal mining region
    same 426-band Tanager instrument over an open-pit mining scene; shortcuts jump straight to the visible bands, red edge, NIR and SWIR
  • EMIT L2A hyperspectral — 285 bands, Pamlico Sound
    read straight out of an Icechunk v2 repo via icechunk-js, no plain-zarr copy; level 0 is EMIT's own ~60 m GLT grid, with L2A cloud/water mask overlays and click-to-spectrum. Draw a box and the block renders as a 3D image cube — wavelength is the z axis
  • SWOT geostrophic currents — animated, native swath
    Windy-style particle field over one L3 pass: colour carries true speed, pale streaks carry direction. The odd one out — no zarr and no regridding, the curvilinear 2 km swath is searched directly
  • MIOST global geostrophic currents — animated, whole ocean
    the same particle engine taken global: one MIOST/DUACS L4 daily grid (multi-mission altimetry incl. SWOT), 0.125°, int16-quantised. The lattice is regular, so the swath search collapses to index arithmetic + a bilinear blend
  • MIOST currents from multiscale Zarr — WebGL raster + zarr-window particles
    the same day as a 4-level GeoZarr pyramid: the speed raster renders straight from the pyramid in WebGL, and the particles re-fetch only the viewport's chunks at the level the zoom resolves — coarse zooms move ~10× fewer bytes than the flat .bin page

Before/after comparisons against the old L.GridLayer (zarr-maps) approach, kept to show what the WebGL rewrite fixed — not for day-to-day use: swot-leaflet.html (old SWOT page — slow, tile seams) and mur-leaflet.html (old MUR page — first load ~30 s).
